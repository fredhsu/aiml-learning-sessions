# The bounded vocabulary corpus

**Cap: 50 items. Current: 44.** This is the *only* legitimate memorised corpus in this curriculum.

Photography is a Type 2 domain where spaced repetition is close to a trap. The small legitimate role is the declarative substrate — the terms that make a perception communicable and the relations that let you reason in the field without a calculator. Everything else is perceptual and procedural, and cannot be flashcarded.

**Exceeding the cap is a named revision trigger,** not progress. If an item is not used in a shot intent, a critique, or a field decision within a macrocycle, delete it.

Retrieval format matters: these are tested by **producing them in the field under time pressure**, never by recognition. "What does 'open shade' mean" is the wrong question. "Name this condition, now, before you raise the camera" is the right one.

## Light — direction (5)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Front light | Source behind the photographer. Flattens form, saturates colour, kills texture. |
| Side light | Source across the subject. Maximum texture and form; the default for showing surface. |
| Back light | Source behind the subject. Separates subject from background; rims edges; meters badly. |
| Top light | Source overhead. Midday sun. Drops eye sockets into shadow; usually the hardest to use. |
| Bounced / reflected | The effective source is a surface, not the original emitter. Changes hardness and colour together. |

## Light — hardness and contrast (6)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hard light | Small source relative to subject. Sharp-edged shadows, high local contrast. |
| Soft light | Large source relative to subject. Gradual shadow edges. Softness is about *relative size*, not brightness. |
| Contrast ratio | Brightness of the lit side against the shadow side, in stops. The number that decides whether a scene fits. |
| Open shade | Blocked from direct sun but open to a large area of sky. Soft, cool, low-contrast. |
| Overcast | The whole sky becomes the source. Very soft, near-shadowless, flat. |
| Dappled | Broken light through a partial obstruction. Almost always exceeds usable contrast range. |

## Light — colour and time (6)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Colour temperature | The warm–cool axis of a source, in kelvin. Lower is warmer. |
| Mixed light | Two or more sources of different temperature in one frame. Cannot be corrected globally. |
| Golden hour | Low sun near sunrise or sunset. Warm, strongly directional, long shadows, changes fast. |
| Blue hour | After sunset or before sunrise. Cool, soft, brief, and requires long exposures. |
| Specular highlight | A direct reflection of the source. Sets where the frame will clip first. |
| Ambient / available | The light already present, unmodified. This curriculum's north star works in it. |

## Exposure relations (8)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stop | A doubling or halving of light. The unit everything is counted in. |
| Aperture (f-number) | Ratio of focal length to entrance pupil. Full stops: 1.4, 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16, 22. |
| Shutter speed | Duration of exposure. Full stops double: 1/30, 1/60, 1/125, 1/250, 1/500, 1/1000. |
| ISO | Sensitivity/amplification. Doubling is one stop, and costs dynamic range as well as adding noise. |
| Reciprocity | Trading one control against another at constant total exposure. |
| Metering | The camera's estimate of exposure. It assumes an average scene and is wrong whenever the scene is not average. |
| Exposure compensation | A deliberate offset from what the meter chose. |
| Clipping | Data lost at either end. Highlight clipping is unrecoverable; shadow clipping is noise, not absence. |

## Optics (7)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Focal length | Determines angle of view. Does **not** determine perspective. |
| Angle of view | How much of the scene the frame covers, given focal length and sensor size. |
| Equivalent focal length | Focal length scaled by crop factor, for comparing angle of view across formats. |
| Perspective | The relationship between near and far objects. Set **only** by camera position. |
| Depth of field | The zone rendered acceptably sharp. Set by aperture, focal length, distance, and format together. |
| Hyperfocal distance | The focus distance at which depth of field extends to infinity for a given aperture. |
| Plane of focus | The single distance actually in focus. Parallel to the sensor; depth of field is the tolerance around it. |

## Seeing (7)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Subject | What the picture is about, stateable in one clause. |
| Frame edge | The boundary. Every edge either does work or leaks attention. |
| Separation | Making the subject legible against its background, by tone, colour, focus, or position. |
| Value mass | A region of similar tone, read as one shape. What survives at thumbnail size. |
| Notan | The arrangement of light and dark masses independent of detail or colour. |
| Gesture | The line of movement or attitude through a subject. |
| Negative space | The shape of the emptiness. It is a shape and it composes. |

## Process and evidence (5)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shot intent | The pre-shutter commitment. Without it a frame is exposure, not evidence. |
| Keeper rate | Frames meeting their own recorded intent ÷ frames made. Never a measure of quality. |
| Calibration gap | Predicted minus actual. The highest-integrity number available in the perceptual half. |
| Blocked constraint | The one variable held constant across an outing so a pattern can stabilise. |
| Contrast pair | The same scene shot two ways with a committed prediction first. The remedy for `M`. |

## Deliberately excluded

Do not add these. Each is either recognition-level, better learned by doing, or an active distraction:

- Named compositional rules as a system — rule of thirds, golden ratio, leading lines. `V1`–`V5` teach the underlying jobs; rule inventories are a substitute for looking.
- Lens and sensor technology terms beyond the seven optics items above.
- Flash and strobe vocabulary — out of scope until a project requires it.
- Colour-space and colour-management terminology beyond one fixed reference viewing condition.
- Camera-model and brand terminology of any kind.
- Genre and movement labels. Useful for reading about photography; useless in the field.
